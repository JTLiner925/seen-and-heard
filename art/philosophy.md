# Mustard Seed

*An algorithmic philosophy for the Seen & Heard hero*

Mustard Seed treats the map as soil and every mark as a single life. Thousands of points are scattered over the land with a seeded, blue-noise sampling: no two closer than their share of the ground allows, so the world reads as an even field of people rather than a pattern. The same seed always lays down the same field, point for point, because every choice the system makes is drawn from one deterministic stream. Change the seed and a different generation stands on the same continents.

Nothing lights at once. The light begins at one small place on the eastern edge of the Mediterranean and moves outward the way a rumour moves: a front that is fast across open ground and slow where it meets resistance. Each point's moment of lighting is the shortest path from any lit source, so the front bends, pools and races without being told to. A soft value-noise field roughens its edge so the spread never reads as a ripple drawn with a compass.

The system also sends. A handful of points, chosen by the seed and weighted toward the hardest places, light early and become sources of their own, so the field fills from several centres at once instead of one ever-widening ring. Where a country's share of unreached people is high, the front slows, and those places are the last to light. The piece is honest about the order in which this has happened.

The band between ten and forty degrees north carries the most weight. Its points are larger and their light is brighter, while the rest of the world stays present but quiet, a dim context for the window where most of the unreached live. Colour is used once: a single accent for the lit band, a quiet ink for everything else, and a brief flash at the moment each point turns.

The viewer is part of the work. Near the pointer, points swell and shrink in a slow ripple, as if the field notices whoever is looking at it. When the spread is complete the field rests and moves only in answer to a hand, so the page stays calm under its words. Every parameter (the seed, the speed of the front, the number of points and the palette) has been tuned through many passes, so the result can hold steady behind a headline as well as stand alone as an image.
